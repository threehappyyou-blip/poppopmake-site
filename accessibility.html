Accessibility

Accessibility Statement

The target is WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Here's what that's meant in practice, what's actually been checked, and — just as important — what hasn't been checked yet. No overlay plugin, no automated badge; just a plain account of the work.

What's been done

  • ✓Every text and background color pair on the site had its contrast ratio calculated directly, not estimated by eye. Anything under 4.5:1 for normal text was darkened until it passed — this covers body text, links, button labels, and small tag labels.
  • ✓A "Skip to main content" link is the first focusable thing on every page, for keyboard and screen-reader users who don't want to tab through the header every time.
  • ✓Every input has a real, associated <label> — not just a placeholder standing in for one.
  • ✓Icon-only buttons (the little ↑ ↓ × controls for reordering files) carry an aria-label, not just a hover tooltip.
  • ✓Headings step down in order (h1 → h2 → h3) with nothing skipped, page by page, so screen-reader users navigating by heading get an accurate outline.
  • ✓Focus is always visible — every interactive element gets a clear outline when reached by keyboard, and it isn't suppressed anywhere.
  • ✓Motion respects prefers-reduced-motion where the site animates anything.

What hasn't been done yet

This is the honest part. Everything above was checked systematically in code — contrast math, label associations, heading order. None of it has been walked through with an actual screen reader (VoiceOver, NVDA, JAWS) by someone who uses one daily, which is the only way to catch the things code review can't: awkward reading order, a control that's technically labeled but confusingly so, a live region that announces at the wrong moment. That testing hasn't happened yet. If you use assistive technology and something here doesn't work the way it should, that's exactly the kind of report that's most useful — see below.

Where this matters most: the document tools involve genuinely visual tasks — cropping a photo, checking a background-removal result — that don't reduce to a fully non-visual equivalent. The calculators, career tools, and writing tools are the ones most likely to work well end-to-end with a screen reader.

Report a problem

If a tool doesn't work with your keyboard, your screen reader, or anything else — that's a bug, and it goes on the Changelog once it's fixed. Tell us what happened and what you were trying to do.
