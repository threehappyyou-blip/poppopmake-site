Tab 1

Age Calculator

Works out exact age in years, months, and days — as of today or as of any other date — along with the total days lived and the days left until the next birthday.

  1. Enter the date of birth in the "Date of birth" field — type it or pick it from the calendar. The field starts at January 1, 2000, so replace it with the real date.
  2. Leave "Calculate age as of" on today, or pick another date to see the age on that day — for example, how old someone will be at a future event or was at a past one. Clearing the field goes back to today.
  3. Read the result. The large line shows the age as years, months, and days (for example 36y 4m 29d), and the line under it writes the same age out in words.
  4. Check the three chips below it: "Total days lived", "Days to next birthday" (it says "Today!" on the birthday itself), and "Born on a" (the weekday of the birth date).
Good to know: The same steps give the time between any two dates: enter the earlier date as the date of birth and the later one as "Calculate age as of". A future "as of" date shows the age on that day — it is not a countdown to an event — and "Days to next birthday" is counted from the "as of" date. A birth date after the "as of" date shows "That birth date is in the future", and an incomplete date shows "Enter a valid date". A February 29 birthday counts as March 1 in years that are not leap years.

Tab 2

Percentage Calculator

Answers four everyday percentage questions: what is X% of Y, X is what percent of Y, the percent change from one number to another, and a number increased or decreased by a percentage.

  1. Pick the question with the four buttons at the top: "X% of Y", "X is what % of Y", "% change, X to Y", or "Increase/decrease by %".
  2. Fill in the two boxes that appear for that question: "Percentage" and "Of this number"; "This number" and "Is what % of this"; "From" and "To"; or "Starting number" and "Percentage". Each question starts with example numbers, so type over them.
  3. For "Increase/decrease by %", choose "Increase" or "Decrease" under the boxes (Increase is selected first).
  4. Read the result in the large box — it updates as you type — with a one-line explanation under it, such as "Increase from 100 to 150" or "100 increased by 20% = 120".
Good to know: "% change, X to Y" divides by the starting number, so going from 10 to 15 is +50%, while going from 15 back to 10 is -33.33%, not -50%. A change from 0, or "X is what % of 0", cannot be written as a percentage, so the result says "Undefined" and explains why instead of showing a made-up number. This calculator works with percent change, not percentage points (the 5-point gap between 10% and 15%).

Tab 3

Tip & Split Calculator

Works out the tip and each person's share of a restaurant bill, with an optional sales-tax line and a choice of tipping on the subtotal or on the total after tax.

  1. Enter the "Subtotal (before tax)" — the amount for the food and drink on the receipt, before tax.
  2. Enter the "Sales tax" rate as a percent if you want tax in the breakdown. It starts at 0, which leaves the tax row out.
  3. Set the "Tip percentage": press 15%, 18%, 20%, or 25%, or type your own number. The highlighted button always matches the number in the box.
  4. Choose what the tip is calculated on: "Subtotal (before tax)" or "Total (after tax)".
  5. Enter the "Number of people" sharing the bill.
  6. Read the table. It has a row each for Subtotal, Tax (only when the sales tax is above 0), Tip, and Total, with the amount for the whole table in the "Total" column and each person's share in "Per person".
Good to know: When an amount does not divide evenly to the cent, the shares are split so they still add up exactly: "$21.12×2 + $21.13×2" means two people pay $21.12 and two pay $21.13. Tipping on the total also tips on the tax itself; most etiquette guides suggest tipping on the subtotal. Fewer than 1 person counts as 1 person, a fraction is dropped (2.9 counts as 2), and a negative amount counts as 0.

Tab 4

Password Generator

Creates a random password of 6 to 64 characters from the character types you choose.

  1. Set the length with the slider. The label above it shows the number ("Length: 16 characters").
  2. Tick the character types to include: Uppercase (A-Z), Lowercase (a-z), Numbers (0-9), and Symbols (!@#$...). A new password appears every time you change the length or a checkbox.
  3. Click "↻ Regenerate" for another password with the same settings.
  4. Click "Copy" to put the password on your clipboard. The button says "Copied!" for a moment.
Good to know: Every type you tick appears in the password at least once, so it also satisfies sites that insist on a capital letter, a number, and a symbol. The password is made in your browser with its secure random number generator and is never sent anywhere; reload the page and it is gone. If your browser refuses to copy, the password is selected for you with a note to press Ctrl+C (⌘C on a Mac). With no box ticked, the page says "Select at least one character type".

Tab 5

Unit Converter

Converts height, weight, temperature, distance, and volume between metric and US units — all five conversions are on the page at once.

  1. Find the card you need: Height (centimeters ↔ feet and inches), Weight (pounds ↔ kilograms), Temperature (°F ↔ °C), Length / Distance (kilometers ↔ miles), or Volume (liters ↔ US gallons).
  2. Type a value in either box of that card. For Height that means centimeters, or feet and inches (the inches box accepts decimals).
  3. Read the other box. It updates as you type. Each card works in both directions, so there is no "from" and "to" to choose.
Good to know: Results are rounded: weight, distance, and volume to 2 decimals; centimeters, inches, and temperature to 1 decimal. Heights carry over properly — 182.8 cm shows as 6 ft 0 in, never "5 ft 12 in". Clearing a box sets the other one to 0. Gallons are US gallons (3.785 liters), not UK gallons.

Tab 6

Time Zone Converter

Shows what one moment looks like in 11 time zones at once, and flags the zones where it falls on a different date than yours.

  1. Enter the date and time in "Date and time (your local time)". It starts at the current moment. Enter it in your own time zone, not the other person's.
  2. Read the table. It lists Pacific (US), Mountain (US), Central (US), Eastern (US), UTC, London, Paris, Seoul, Tokyo, Shanghai, and Sydney, each with the local time and a short zone name such as PDT or GMT+9.
  3. Look for "(+1 day)" or "(-1 day)" after a time. It means that in that zone the moment falls on the next or the previous calendar day compared with the date you entered.
Good to know: Daylight saving time is applied for the date you pick, so the same zone can be an hour apart in summer and winter. The 11 zones are fixed, and clearing the field brings back the prompt to enter a date and time. Check the day as well as the hour when you schedule something across zones — "3 pm your time" can already be tomorrow morning somewhere else.
