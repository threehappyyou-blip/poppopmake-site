Tab 1

Age Calculator

Calculates exact age — or the time between any two dates — down to years, months, and days.

  1. Enter a birth date (or the earlier of two dates you're comparing) using the date field.
  2. Enter the date to calculate to — today's date is often filled in by default, but you can change it to any other date.
  3. Read the result: the exact number of years, months, and days between the two dates.
Good to know: This also works for calculating the time until a future date, not just age from a past birth date — enter a future date as the "to" date to see a countdown instead.

Tab 2

Percentage Calculator

Handles three common percentage questions: what is X% of Y, what percent is X of Y, and percent change between two numbers.

  1. Choose which type of question you're asking, using the tabs or toggle for each mode.
  2. Enter your numbers into the two fields for that mode.
  3. Read the result, which updates live as you type.
Good to know: "Percent change" and "percentage points" are different things this tool distinguishes correctly — going from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage-point increase, but a 50% relative increase. Check which one you actually mean before quoting a figure.

Tab 3

Tip & Split Calculator

Splits a restaurant bill among any number of people, with a choice of tipping on the subtotal or the after-tax total.

  1. Enter the subtotal (the amount before tax, from your receipt).
  2. Enter the sales tax rate if you want tax included in the breakdown.
  3. Choose a tip percentage using the preset buttons (15%, 18%, 20%, 25%) or type a custom amount.
  4. Choose what the tip is calculated on — subtotal (before tax) or total (after tax). Most etiquette guides suggest tipping on the subtotal.
  5. Enter the number of people splitting the bill.
  6. Read the breakdown: subtotal, tax, tip, and total, both overall and per person.
Good to know: Tipping on the total instead of the subtotal means you're also tipping on the tax itself — a small difference, but worth knowing which one you're actually doing.

Tab 4

Password Generator

Generates a random, secure password using your chosen length and character types.

  1. Set the length using the slider or number field — longer is generally more secure.
  2. Choose which character types to include — uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols, using the checkboxes.
  3. Read the generated password, shown in a field at the top.
  4. Click "Copy" to copy it to your clipboard, or click "Generate" again for a new one if you don't like the first result.
Good to know: The password is generated using your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator, not a simple pseudo-random one — and since nothing is uploaded anywhere, the password never exists outside your own device until you copy and use it yourself.

Tab 5

Unit Converter

Converts between units of height, weight, and temperature.

  1. Choose a category — height, weight, or temperature.
  2. Choose your "from" unit (like centimeters, pounds, or Celsius) and enter your value.
  3. Choose your "to" unit (like feet and inches, kilograms, or Fahrenheit).
  4. Read the converted value, which updates live as you type.
Good to know: Height conversions to feet and inches round correctly at the edges — for example, a value that rounds up to a full extra foot displays as that next foot with zero inches, not as an incorrect "12 inches" within the previous foot.

Tab 6

Time Zone Converter

Shows what one specific moment in time looks like across 11 different time zones at once.

  1. Enter a date and time in your own time zone.
  2. Read the converted times shown for all 11 zones at once, side by side.
  3. Check the day-offset flags. Zones where the converted time falls on a different calendar day (the day before or after) are marked clearly, since this is the detail that's easy to miss and causes the most scheduling mistakes.
Good to know: Always double-check the day, not just the time, when scheduling something across time zones — a meeting that's "3pm your time" might already be tomorrow morning somewhere else, and the day-offset flags exist specifically to catch that.
