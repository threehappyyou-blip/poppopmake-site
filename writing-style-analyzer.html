How to analyze your writing style in 3 steps

  1. Paste at least 150 words. A single paragraph gives a noisy average; a few paragraphs from the same piece show your real habits.
  2. Read the rhythm chart first. It answers the question most style reports skip: does your writing have a pulse, or does every sentence run the same length?
  3. Compare against the column that matches your goal. A blog post, a cover letter and a term paper have different healthy ranges, so the same number can be fine in one and a problem in another.

The readability formulas are the published ones (Flesch 1948, Kincaid 1975, Gunning 1952). Syllables are counted with a standard rule-based method, so an unusual word can be off by one, but over a few paragraphs those misses average out.

Reading the sentence rhythm chart

Good prose usually looks like a skyline, not a fence. A run of five or more bars at almost the same height is where readers start to drift, even if every sentence is grammatical. The fix is rarely to rewrite everything: split one long sentence, or merge two short ones, inside the flat stretch. A single very tall orange bar is a sentence that probably needs to become two.

A quick before-and-after

Before (Flesch 38, 31 words, passive): “It was decided by the committee that, due to the fact that attendance had been very low, the event would basically be postponed until a later date could be determined.”

After (Flesch 78, two sentences, active): “Attendance was low, so the committee postponed the event. They’ll set a new date soon.”

Paste either version into the analyzer to watch the numbers move: the passive construction, the filler (“basically”), the wordy phrase (“due to the fact that”) and the single long sentence all disappear in the rewrite.

Frequently asked questions

How can I analyze my writing style for free?

Paste your text into the box above and press “Analyze my style”. You get sentence rhythm, readability, passive voice, adverbs, vocabulary variety and your most repeated words instantly, with no account and no word limit, because everything runs in your browser.

What is a good Flesch reading ease score?

For most web pages and business writing, 60–70 is the plain-English sweet spot — roughly what a 13- to 15-year-old reads comfortably. Above 80 is very easy (good for fiction and casual posts); below 50 is hard going and typical of academic papers and legal text.

How long should a sentence be?

Aim for an average of about 15–20 words, but vary it. A mix of short and long sentences reads better than every sentence sitting at 18 words. Any sentence over 35 words is worth splitting.

Is passive voice always wrong?

No. Passive voice is useful when the doer is unknown or unimportant (“the bridge was built in 1932”) and is normal in scientific methods sections. It becomes a problem when it hides who did what or appears in more than about one in ten sentences of everyday writing.

Is my text saved or sent anywhere?

No. The analyzer is a small script that runs on this page in your browser. Nothing you paste is uploaded, logged or stored, and it keeps working offline once the page has loaded.
