Budget, Notes & Diary (app)

The Budget Tracker, Sticky Notes and Private Diary are three tabs of one app at poppopmake.com/vault, kept only on your device and locked with your passcode. The app screens are in English, Korean or Japanese.

Tab 1 · Budget

Budget Tracker

Records what you spend and earn, shows how much of your monthly budget is left and where the money went, and keeps everything encrypted on your device — no account and no bank connection.

  1. Open the app. Go to poppopmake.com/vault, or tap "Open the app" on the Budget Tracker page. It starts in your phone's language (English, Korean or Japanese); the Language menu changes it.
  2. Set up the lock (first time only). Tap "Get started", type a passcode of at least 6 characters twice, check the currency, tick the box and tap "Continue".
  3. Save your recovery code. Copy it or tap "Save as a file" and keep it somewhere other than this phone. Tick "I’ve saved my recovery code somewhere safe" and tap "Done". A short feature tour follows; tap "Next" or "Skip".
  4. Add spending in three taps. Tap the orange +, type the amount and tap a category — the entry is saved at once. Type a note or change the date first if you want; for money coming in, tap "Income" first.
  5. Add it up in the amount box. Type a sum such as 12000+3500 or 4500×3, or tap the + − × ÷ buttons under the box; the result appears below it and is what gets saved.
  6. Start from your home screen. After installing the app, press and hold its icon and choose "Add spending": the add screen opens right after you unlock.
  7. Use your frequent entries. Anything you have logged at least twice in the last 90 days with the same amount, category and note appears as a button at the top of the add screen; one tap adds it for today.
  8. Fix or delete an entry. Tap it in the list, change it and tap "Save", or tap "Delete". "Undo" in the message at the bottom brings back an entry you just added or deleted.
  9. Set budgets. On the Budget tab tap "Set a monthly budget", or use Settings → Monthly budget. Settings → Category budgets sets a limit for single categories. With a monthly budget, the card also shows how much you can spend today: what is left, shared out over the days left in the month. Repeating expenses still to come this month (rent, subscriptions) are kept aside first, and the card says so.
  10. Save up for something. Tap "Start a saving goal" on the budget card (or "Goals"), then "New goal": choose an emoji, a name, the target amount and, if you like, a date — with a date you see how much to put in each month. Open the goal and tap "Put in" whenever you save, or "Take out". The app cheers at a quarter, half and three quarters, and when you reach the target it celebrates and moves the goal to "Goals reached". Money in goals is kept apart from spending, so it never makes "Spent" bigger.
  11. Repeat rent or subscriptions. Tick "Repeat every month on this day" while adding the entry. It is then added automatically each month on that day; Settings → Repeating entries pauses or deletes it.
  12. Note how you paid (optional). Settings → Payment methods: tap "Cash", "Card" or "Bank transfer", or type a name such as your card’s and tap "Add". The add screen then shows them as buttons under the date; tap one before the category, and it is remembered for next time. Charts shows "By payment method" for the month. Deleting a method keeps its entries.
  13. Keep a receipt. On the add screen tap "Receipt photo" before the category and take or pick a photo; it is shrunk and encrypted like diary photos. Entries with a receipt show 🧾; open the entry and tap the picture to see it full size.
  14. See where the money went. Tap "Charts" for spending by category, category budgets and the last six months; "See the whole year" shows the year’s spending, income, monthly average, a bar for each month (tap one to open it) and the categories for the year. The arrows beside the month switch months, and the magnifier searches notes and categories in every month.
  15. Unlock with your fingerprint or face (optional). Settings → Unlock with fingerprint or face, type your passcode once and follow your phone’s prompt. From then on the fingerprint or face prompt appears by itself when you open the app or come back to it while it is locked (not right after you tap the padlock); cancel it to type your passcode instead, or untick "Ask for fingerprint or face as soon as the app opens" under that setting. The lock screen also keeps the "Unlock with fingerprint or face" button; your passcode keeps working. It needs a recent phone or computer (for example iPhone with iOS 18.4 or later, Android with Chrome, Windows 11 with Windows Hello); where it can’t work, the setting is not shown or says so.
  16. Lock it. Tap the padlock at the top. The app also locks itself after the time set in Settings → Lock automatically after (5 minutes unless you change it), including when you switch away from the app. “Never (lock it yourself)” turns this off — then anyone using the device can read your records until you lock the app or close the browser, so choose it only on a computer that only you use.
Good to know: Your data is stored only in this browser on this device, so make a backup: Settings → Back up to a file. On a new phone, open the app and tap "I have a backup file" (or "Restore from a backup file" on the lock screen), then enter the passcode used when the backup was made, or your recovery code. Forgot your passcode? Tap "Forgot your passcode?" and enter your recovery code; without it nobody, including PopPopMake, can open the data. Settings → Import a bank statement (CSV) reads your bank's file on your device and skips entries you already have; Export to a spreadsheet (CSV) creates a file that is not encrypted. Changing the currency does not convert entries you already have. Category emoji are chosen from a picker in Settings → Categories (or typed with "Or type your own"). Fingerprint / face unlock keeps a passkey in your phone’s own password manager (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager or Windows Hello); nothing is sent to PopPopMake, and turning it off in the app leaves the passkey there for you to delete. The app keeps records; it does not give financial advice.
Tab 2 · Notes

Sticky Notes

Keeps colored sticky notes — shopping lists, ideas, reminders — under the same passcode lock as the budget, and on a computer puts them on your desktop in their own small windows.

  1. Open Notes. In the app, tap "Notes" at the bottom, or open poppopmake.com/vault?start=notes.
  2. Write a note. Tap the orange + and type. The note saves as you type; close it with the × button or the Esc key.
  3. Pick a color, lettering and size. Tap one of the six pastel color dots under the note. "Lettering" switches between Standard, Handwriting and Pen, and "Text size" between Small, Medium and Large.
  4. Make a checklist. Tap "Checklist" in a note: each line becomes a tick box. Press Enter for the next item and tick items off as you go; the card shows how many are done. "Plain text" switches back.
  5. Lock a single note. Tap "Lock this note". Its card then shows only "Locked note", search skips it, and opening it asks for your passcode every time — even while the app is unlocked. "Remove the note lock" turns it off.
  6. Add photos. Tap "Photo" under the note to add up to six pictures (a whiteboard, a parking spot); the card shows the first one. A note with only photos is kept too.
  7. Get reminded. Tap "Reminder" in a note, choose the date and time, then "Add to my calendar (file)" or "Add to Google Calendar". The note’s first line becomes the title and its text the description, unless you tick "Don’t put the note’s text in the calendar"; a locked note never puts its text there. The card shows ⏰ with the time.
  8. Pin what matters. Tap "Pin to top" so the note stays first; "Unpin" releases it.
  9. Find a note. Type in "Search notes" above your notes.
  10. Delete a note. Open it and tap "Delete". Tap "Undo" straight away if it was a mistake.
  11. Put a note on your computer’s desktop. In Chrome or Edge on a computer, open a note and click "Put on desktop". It opens in its own small window that you can move and resize anywhere on the screen; what you type there appears in the app at once, and the other way round. In that window, + makes another desktop note, the list button ("Back to the list") takes it off the desktop, and the padlock ("Lock all") locks every window of the app.
  12. Keep one note on top. Click "Keep on top" (in the note, or the pin button in a note window). The note moves to a small window that stays above every other window, and the window it came from shrinks to a thin bar. Click 📌 in the on-top window (or "Stop keeping on top" in the bar) to put it back. Leave the bar open; you can minimise it.
  13. Line them up. Drag a note window close to another one or to the edge of the screen and let go: it sticks to it. "Tidy desktop notes" on the Notes tab lines every open note window up on the right of the screen. Untick "Snap note windows to each other and to the screen edges" to turn sticking off.
  14. Open your desktop notes again. After you unlock the app on that computer, your desktop notes reopen by themselves. If the browser opens only the first one, allow pop-ups for poppopmake.com (the icon at the right end of the address bar) and click "Open desktop notes" on the Notes tab.
  15. Start them with the computer (optional). Install the app, then follow Settings → Open faster on this computer (see Open it faster). Your desktop notes reopen after you unlock. To keep the note windows unlocked, set Settings → Lock automatically after → Never on a computer only you use.
Good to know: A note left empty is not kept. Notes are encrypted together with your budget and travel in the same backup file. Desktop note windows show the same notes as the app in the same browser on the same computer; they do not sync to your phone by themselves (move everything with Settings → Move to another device (QR) or a backup file), and "Keep on top" needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. For important passwords, a dedicated password manager with its own recovery options is still the safer choice.
Tab 3 · Diary

Private Diary

Keeps a diary with a mood, photos and #tags for each entry — decorated with stickers, washi tape, paper, handwriting, drawings and week, hourly or to-do pages — plus a calendar of moods, your writing streak and what you wrote on this day in earlier years, under the same lock and in the same backup file.

  1. Open the Diary. In the app, tap "Diary" at the bottom, or open poppopmake.com/vault?start=diary.
  2. Write an entry. Tap the orange +, or "Write" under today’s question. The date and time are filled in; type about your day and it saves as you type.
  3. Pick a mood. Tap one of the five faces; tap the same face again to clear it.
  4. Add photos. Tap "Add photo" and choose up to 4 pictures. They are made smaller and encrypted before they are saved. Tap a photo to see it full size, or the × ("Remove photo") to take it out.
  5. Group entries with #tags. Write words such as #travel in the text; the tags appear under the text and in search.
  6. Browse. "List" shows entries by day; "Calendar" shows each day's mood — tap a day to open it or to start an entry for that day. The arrows beside the month switch months.
  7. Look back. "On this day" shows entries from the same date in earlier years, and the card at the top shows how many days in a row you have written.
  8. Search. Tap the magnifier and type words or a #tag.
  9. Decorate the page. In an entry, tap "Paper" for plain, lined, grid or dotted paper and one of seven colours, and "Lettering" for a handwriting or pen font.
  10. Add stickers and washi tape. Tap "Stickers", choose a sticker, a tape or an emoji, then drag it where you like. Tap a sticker for its buttons: smaller, bigger, turn left or right, bring to front and remove. On a keyboard, the arrow keys move a selected sticker and Delete removes it. Up to 60 per entry.
  11. Use a planner page. Tap "Page" and choose "Week" (seven day boxes and a habit tracker with a tick for each day), "Hourly" (06 to 24) or "To-do" (press Enter for the next item). Switching pages keeps what you wrote on each; search finds words on them too.
  12. Draw. Tap "Draw", pick a colour and line width and draw with your finger or mouse; "Eraser", "Undo" and "Clear" fix mistakes. Tap "Done" to keep the drawing.
  13. Make it a book or a PDF. Tap "Save this month as a PDF / print" under a month’s entries, or use Settings → Diary as a PDF or on paper to choose one month, one year or everything, with or without photos. Tap "Open the print screen": the entries are laid out by month with their date, mood, paper, stickers, drawings and photos, and your device’s print screen opens, where you print or choose "Save as PDF". The PDF is not encrypted.
  14. Delete an entry. Open it and tap "Delete". "Undo" right afterwards brings it back.
Good to know: An entry with no text, photo or mood is not kept. Photos stay encrypted on your device and travel, still encrypted, in the backup file. Settings → Save the diary as a text file creates a file that is not encrypted, and photos are not in it. Decorations and drawings are encrypted with the entry and travel in the backup file. The stickers and paper designs are original PopPopMake drawings; the handwriting fonts (Patrick Hand, Gaegu, Yomogi, Caveat, Hi Melody, Klee One) are used under the SIL Open Font License, and their files load only when you choose them.

The helper: suggestions, questions and step-by-step help

Suggests what is worth doing today, finds any feature from a few words of your own, and walks you through a task on the real buttons, so there is nothing to learn up front. It works only from what is already on your device, offline too, and sends nothing anywhere.

  1. Today’s suggestions. At the top of Budget (and of Notes and Diary, for their own things) up to three cards appear when there is something worth doing: a payment due in the next three days, a note reminder today, a day you forgot to write down, a budget going faster than the month, last month’s total in the first days of a month, a payment that comes every month but isn’t set to repeat yet, one line in the diary in the evening, or a feature you haven’t tried. The first button does it: "Write it now" opens Add already set to yesterday, and "Make it repeat" turns that monthly payment into a repeating entry in one tap (with "Undo").
  2. Put one off or turn it off. "Not today" (or "I didn’t spend anything") hides that card until tomorrow; "Hide for today" hides them all; "Don’t suggest this again" stops that kind of suggestion. Settings → Helper → "Show the suggestions I turned off" brings them back, and "Show today’s suggestions" switches the cards off altogether.
  3. Ask in your own words. Tap 💁 at the top of any tab and type what you want, for example ‘receipt photo’, ‘new phone’, ‘back up’ or ‘yesterday’s spending’. Short words, typos, no spaces, Korean initials (ㅇㅅㅈ) and a mix of languages all work. Tap "Open" to go straight to it, or "Show me how" to be walked through it; press Enter for the first answer.
  4. Step-by-step help on the real buttons. A ring shows the button to press and a short note says what to do; each step moves on by itself when you do it, and "Stop" ends it. The note never covers what you need to tap. Settings → Helper lists the walk-throughs: writing down spending, a receipt photo, a note, a diary entry, a monthly budget, a backup, importing a bank CSV, and a reminder for a note.
  5. Help when you seem stuck. If a new entry, note or diary page stays empty for 20 seconds, or saving fails twice, a note offers "Show me" or "I’m fine". Once you have done something on your own twice, it stops offering help for it. Turn it off with "Offer step-by-step help when I seem stuck" in Settings → Helper.
Good to know: What the helper remembers (cards put off, kinds turned off, things you have done) is kept with your settings, encrypted with everything else, and goes along in backups.

Open it faster, and move it to another device

Installs the app so it opens from an icon instead of a website, unlocks with your fingerprint as soon as it opens, starts with your computer, backs itself up to a folder, reminds you every day, takes text shared from other apps, has keyboard shortcuts on a computer, and moves everything to a new phone or computer with QR codes.

  1. Install it as an app. Until you install it, the lock screen and the top of the app show "Open it in one tap next time". Tap "Install as an app": on Android or a computer with Chrome or Edge, tap "Install"; on iPhone or iPad, open the page in Safari, tap the Share button and choose "Add to Home Screen". "Not now" hides the card; Settings → Install as an app shows the steps again.
  2. Unlock without typing. Turn on Settings → Unlock with fingerprint or face once. After that, opening the app shows the fingerprint or face prompt straight away.
  3. Pick up where you left off. The app opens on the tab you used last (Budget, Notes or Diary). Home-screen shortcuts such as "Add spending" still go straight to their screen.
  4. Open it with your own key (computer). Settings → Open faster on this computer shows how. On Windows: Start menu → right-click the app → More → Open file location; right-click the shortcut → Properties → click "Shortcut key" and press a letter (V makes Ctrl+Alt+V) → OK. On a Mac: in the Shortcuts app, make a shortcut that opens the app and give it a keyboard shortcut. That key then opens the app from any program.
  5. Open it when the computer starts. The same Settings page lists the steps: in Chrome, type chrome://apps, right-click the app and turn on "Start app when you sign in" (or "Launch at startup"); in Edge, go to edge://apps, click ⋯ and choose "Auto-start on device login"; with any browser on Windows, press Windows key + R, type shell:startup and drag the app’s Start-menu shortcut into that folder; on a Mac, add the app under System Settings → General → Login Items.
  6. Back up by itself (computer). In Chrome or Edge on a computer, Settings → Automatic backup (computer) → "Choose a folder". Pick your Google Drive, OneDrive or Dropbox folder so the copy also leaves the computer. From then on the app saves an encrypted backup there whenever something changed (it checks every 30 seconds, when you leave the app and when you lock): poppopmake-vault-backup-latest.json plus one file per day, kept for 30 days. If the browser asks again after a restart, tap "Allow" on the card at the top of the app. "Back up now", "Choose another folder" and "Turn off" are in the same place.
  7. Get a reminder every day. Settings → Daily reminder (calendar): choose the time and "Write in my diary", "Log today’s spending" or "Do both". "Add to my calendar (file)" downloads a calendar file (open it and choose Add on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Samsung Calendar or Outlook); "Add to Google Calendar" opens Google Calendar with the reminder filled in. The reminder repeats daily and opens the app on the right screen.
  8. Use the keyboard (computer). When you aren’t typing in a box: N new note, A add spending, D new diary entry, / search, 1 2 3 4 switch to Budget, Notes, Diary and Settings, L lock, ? shows the list (also in Settings → Keyboard shortcuts). They work with Korean or Japanese input switched on.
  9. Save something from another app as a note (Android). In any app, tap Share and choose the installed app (it may be listed as "PopPop Vault"). It opens and, once unlocked, saves the shared text and link as a new note.
  10. Move everything to another device. On the device that has your data, open Settings → Move to another device (QR) → "Send from this device"; a QR code that keeps changing appears, with how many codes there are and about how long it takes. On the other device, open the app and tap "Receive from another device (QR)" on the first screen or the lock screen (or, if it is already in use, Settings → Move to another device (QR) → "Receive on this device"), allow the camera and point it at the codes. Codes you miss come round again. When it says all codes are received, type the sending device’s passcode (or recovery code) and tap "Open and save".
Good to know: Moving with QR codes replaces everything on the receiving device, and the receiving device then uses the sending device’s passcode. Fingerprint or face unlock is not carried over (with QR codes or a backup file); turn it on again on the new device. It is a one-time copy, not automatic syncing. The codes hold only encrypted data and nothing goes over the internet. Text-only data takes seconds to a minute; many photos or drawings can take several minutes, and above about 3 MB the app asks you to use a backup file instead. A computer needs a camera to receive, so send from the computer and receive on the phone; on a computer screen the codes are denser, so it goes quicker. Shared text waits on your device until the app picks it up, which it does as soon as it opens, and is never sent to PopPopMake. Sharing into the app works on Android; iPhone and iPad don’t offer it for web apps. Starting with the computer still asks for your passcode or fingerprint. Automatic backup files are encrypted like any backup; the app remembers only which folder you chose, and folder access isn’t available on phones. The calendar reminder lives in your calendar, so it works without the app open; with Google Calendar, only its title and time go to Google.
Lock & recovery

Forgotten passcode or lost recovery code

What to do when you can’t remember your passcode, what happens if the recovery code is lost as well, and who is responsible for keeping them.

  1. Find your recovery code. It is the 28-character code shown when you set up the app (or the newest one from Settings → Get a new recovery code). If you tapped "Save as a file", look for a text file named like poppopmake-recovery-code.
  2. Open the recovery screen. On the lock screen tap "Forgot your passcode?".
  3. Type the recovery code. Capital or small letters both work, with or without the dashes.
  4. Choose a new passcode. Type a new passcode of at least 6 characters twice and tap "Unlock". Your budget, notes and diary open as before, and the same recovery code keeps working; if someone else may have seen it, use Settings → Get a new recovery code.
  5. Lost the recovery code too? Try a backup. Tap "Lost your recovery code too?" and then "Restore from a backup file". A backup opens with the passcode that was in use when it was made, or with the recovery code you had then, so an older passcode you still remember may open an older backup.
  6. No backup opens. Then nobody can open the data — not you and not PopPopMake. There is no reset by email and no recovery through customer support, because PopPopMake never receives your passcode, recovery code or data. "Start over on this device" erases the locked data so you can set up the app again.
  7. Prevent it. Keep the recovery code on paper or somewhere other than your phone, and make a backup at least once a month (Settings → Back up to a file). Settings → If you forget your passcode shows these steps inside the app.
Your responsibility: Keeping your passcode, recovery code and backup files safe is your responsibility. You confirm this when you set up the app, and it is part of the Terms. Data that can’t be opened because these were lost cannot be restored by PopPopMake.
