Worked example

The default view above: a $70,000/year base salary, paid fortnightly (superannuation is paid on top by your employer and isn't subtracted here). Under the FY2026-27 resident brackets, the first $18,200 is tax-free, then 15% up to $45,000, then 30% up to $135,000 — for gross tax of $11,520. The Low Income Tax Offset doesn't reduce this further at $70,000: LITO tapers down from a maximum of $700 and reaches $0 by $66,667, below this salary. The Medicare levy is a flat 2% here, or $1,400, since $70,000 is above the $35,013 phase-in ceiling. Total tax comes to $12,920, leaving $57,080/year take-home (about $2,195 per fortnightly payslip) — an effective rate of about 18.5%, with a 30% marginal rate on your next dollar.

Frequently asked questions

Do I get the $18,200 tax-free threshold AND the Low Income Tax Offset?

Yes, this calculator applies both — they're separate mechanisms. The $18,200 tax-free threshold is built into the tax brackets themselves. LITO is a separate offset worth up to $700, but it tapers down (5 cents per dollar between $37,500 and $45,000, then 1.5 cents per dollar between $45,000 and $66,667) and disappears completely above $66,667. That's why it doesn't show up in the breakdown at the $70,000 default — LITO has already phased out to $0 by then.

Why is the Medicare levy sometimes less than 2%?

Below $28,011 of income there's no Medicare levy at all. Between $28,011 and $35,013, this calculator applies a reduced rate — 10% of the amount over $28,011 — so the levy phases in gradually instead of jumping straight to 2%. Above $35,013, it's a flat 2% of your full income, which is the band the $70,000 default falls into.

Why might my real payslip show a different number?

This uses FY2026-27 resident tax brackets, LITO, and the standard 2% Medicare levy. It doesn't account for HECS/HELP repayments, salary-sacrificed super, private health insurance and the Medicare Levy Surcharge, or work-related deductions — any of which can change your real take-home. Treat it as a solid estimate, not your exact payslip.

Does this calculator send my salary anywhere?

No. The entire calculation runs in your browser with JavaScript — nothing you type is transmitted, logged, or stored on a server.

Why isn't superannuation subtracted from my take-home pay?

Because the salary you enter is treated as your base salary, with super paid on top by your employer under Australia's compulsory super guarantee — it isn't withheld from your pay the way tax and the Medicare levy are, so this calculator doesn't subtract it.
