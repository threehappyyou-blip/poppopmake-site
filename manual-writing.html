Writing & AI

All 3 tools live on one page, Writing & AI Tools, as tabs. None of these claim to be an "AI detector" — see why on the tool page itself.

Tab 1

Flagged Phrases

Scans your text for common phrases and clichés that read as AI-generated or overly formulaic, and highlights them.

  1. Open the tool. Go to Writing & AI Tools and make sure "Flagged Phrases" is selected.
  2. Paste your text into the box.
  3. Read the highlighted results. Flagged phrases are marked directly in your text as you look at it.
  4. Hover over or click a flagged phrase (if the tool shows this) to see a plain-language suggestion for what to say instead.
  5. Edit your original text based on what feels right — the suggestions are a starting point, not a rule to follow every time.
Good to know: This is a pattern-matching tool that looks for specific known phrases, not a machine-learning AI detector — it can't tell you with certainty whether text was AI-written, and doesn't claim to. It just flags language patterns that commonly read as stiff or formulaic, whoever wrote them.

Tab 2

Smooth It Out

Suggests rewrites for sentences that read as stiff or overly formal.

  1. Open the tool. Click the "Smooth It Out" tab.
  2. Paste your text into the box.
  3. Read the suggested rewrites shown alongside or below the flagged sentences.
  4. Accept or ignore each suggestion by editing your own text — nothing is changed automatically.
Good to know: A suggested rewrite isn't always better for your specific context — some formality is appropriate depending on who you're writing for, so use judgment rather than accepting every suggestion.

Tab 3

Word Count & Readability

Shows a live word and sentence count, plus a Flesch readability score, as you type or paste text.

  1. Open the tool. Click the "Word Count & Readability" tab.
  2. Type or paste your text into the box.
  3. Watch the counts update live — word count and sentence count update as you type, with no button to click.
  4. Read the readability score and what grade level or reading ease it corresponds to.
Good to know: Readability scores are based on sentence length and syllable count, not meaning — a short, simple-sounding sentence about a complex topic can score "easy" while still being hard to actually understand. Use the score as one signal, not the whole picture.
