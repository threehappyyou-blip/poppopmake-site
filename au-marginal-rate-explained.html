Your Real Marginal Tax Rate Isn't What the Bracket Table Says

Australia's official brackets are 15%, 30%, 37%, and 45%. Two other things quietly stack on top of them at specific income ranges, and the result is a real marginal rate that jumps around a lot more than the bracket table lets on.

The two things the bracket table leaves out

The Low Income Tax Offset (LITO) gives you up to $700 back, but it phases out as your income rises — 5 cents for every dollar between $37,500 and $45,000, then 1.5 cents for every dollar between $45,000 and $66,667. A phase-out is a hidden tax: losing 5 cents of offset per extra dollar earned works exactly like an extra 5% tax on that dollar.

The Medicare levy normally sits at a flat 2%, but there's a "shade-in" zone between $28,011 and $35,013 where it ramps up from 0% to 2% at 10 cents per dollar — five times steeper than the flat rate on either side of it.

What this actually does to your marginal rate

We ran the numbers through our own calculator's engine in $1,000 steps to find where the real rate actually changes, not just where the official brackets say it should:

Income rangeWhat's happeningReal marginal rate
$30,000 – $35,00015% bracket + Medicare shade-in25.0%
$35,000 – $37,50015% bracket, shade-in ending17.1%
$37,500 – $45,00015% bracket + LITO taper (5c)22.0%
$45,000 – $66,66730% bracket + LITO taper (1.5c)33.5%
$67,000 – $135,00030% bracket, offsets exhausted32.0%

The gap is biggest right at the bottom: someone earning between $30,000 and $35,000 is often told they're "in the 15% bracket." Their real marginal rate on that next dollar is 25% — ten points higher, entirely from the Medicare shade-in zone that doesn't show up on any bracket table.

Why this matters for decisions, not just curiosity

If you're deciding whether extra overtime, a side gig, or a small raise is worth it, the bracket table alone will make it look better than it is. Someone at $44,000 sizing up a raise to $46,000 based on "I'm in the 30% bracket now" is actually looking at 33.5% on that increase, not 30% — a real difference on take-home pay from the same raise.
