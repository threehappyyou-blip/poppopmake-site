Scotland vs England Income Tax (2026/27): What Actually Differs

2026/27 tax year · recalculated October 2026

Scotland taxes income differently from the rest of the UK — and not in a simple "higher" or "lower" way. Whether that helps you or costs you depends entirely on how much you earn.

The short answer for 2026/27:

  • Below a salary of about £33,500, you keep slightly more in Scotland — at most about £40 a year.
  • Above it, you keep less, and the gap grows fast: £1,496 at £50,000, £3,300 at £100,000, £5,931 at £150,000.
  • National Insurance is identical everywhere in the UK; the whole difference is Income Tax.

Scotland has six bands. Everyone else has three.

Income tax was partly devolved to the Scottish Parliament in 2016, and Scotland has used that power to build a more finely-graded system than the rest of the UK:

What doesn't change: National Insurance

National Insurance is a UK-wide, reserved matter — it's calculated identically whether you're in Edinburgh or Exeter. Only Income Tax has the England/Wales/NI vs Scotland split. That means the full difference between the two systems shows up entirely in the Income Tax line, not anywhere else on a payslip.

Who actually comes out ahead

Because Scotland's lower bands are slightly less than 20% and its higher bands considerably more, the system genuinely reverses direction as income rises:

At the very bottom of the income scale, Scotland's 19% starter rate makes it marginally cheaper. The two systems cost the same at a salary of about £33,500; above that it flips, and it keeps widening from there — by the time you're earning £150,000, the gap is nearly six thousand pounds a year. There's no single honest answer to "is Scottish tax higher" — it depends entirely on where your income falls.

Common questions

Is income tax higher in Scotland than in England?

It depends on your salary. For 2026/27, someone earning less than about £33,500 pays slightly less Income Tax in Scotland (at most about £40 a year). Above that, Scotland costs more: about £1,496 more at £50,000 and £5,931 more at £150,000.

At what salary does Scotland become more expensive?

About £33,500 a year in 2026/27. Below it, the 19% starter rate saves a little; above it, the 21% intermediate rate and the 42% higher rate, which starts at a lower income than in England, outweigh that saving.

Is National Insurance different in Scotland?

No. National Insurance is set UK-wide and is the same in Scotland, England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Only Income Tax differs.

Which tax do I pay if I live in Scotland but work in England?

Scottish Income Tax depends on where you live, not where you work. If your main home is in Scotland you are a Scottish taxpayer and HMRC gives you a tax code starting with S.

Does the Personal Allowance work the same in Scotland?

Yes. The £12,570 Personal Allowance, and its withdrawal between £100,000 and £125,140, are the same across the UK.
