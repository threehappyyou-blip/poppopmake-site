Tab 1

Resume Builder

Builds a clean, single-column resume as a downloadable PDF, formatted to be easy for both people and applicant tracking systems to read.

  1. Open the tool. Go to Career Tools and make sure "Resume Builder" is selected.
  2. Fill in your contact information — name, email, phone, location.
  3. Add your work experience. Click "Add job" for each position, filling in the company, title, dates, and a few bullet points describing what you did.
  4. Add your education the same way — school, degree, dates.
  5. Add skills as a list.
  6. Reorder sections if needed by dragging them, if the tool offers drag handles for section order.
  7. Preview to see how it looks as you go.
  8. Click "Download PDF" once you're happy with it.
Good to know: The single-column layout is a deliberate choice — multi-column resumes can confuse automated resume-scanning software (ATS) into reading text out of order, which single-column formats avoid entirely.

Tab 2

ATS Score Checker

Compares your resume's text against a specific job description's keywords, showing which important terms are missing.

  1. Open the tool. Click the "ATS Score Checker" tab.
  2. Paste your resume text into the first box — a plain-text copy works fine, you don't need to upload a file.
  3. Paste the job description into the second box, copied from the job posting.
  4. Click "Check Match."
  5. Read the results. You'll see a match score and a list of keywords from the job description that don't appear in your resume.
  6. Adjust your resume based on genuinely relevant missing keywords, then re-check if you want to see the updated score.
Good to know: This is a keyword-matching tool, not a simulation of any specific company's actual ATS software — those vary widely and their exact scoring logic isn't public. Treat a low score as "worth a look," not a verdict on whether you'll get an interview.

Tab 3

Cover Letter Generator

Assembles a one-page cover letter PDF from your own answers to a few prompts — it doesn't write content for you, it formats what you provide.

  1. Open the tool. Click the "Cover Letter Generator" tab.
  2. Fill in the company name and role you're applying for.
  3. Fill in your qualifications — a few sentences or bullet points about why you're a fit, in your own words.
  4. Fill in your contact information.
  5. Preview the assembled letter.
  6. Click "Download PDF."
Good to know: If you leave the qualifications section blank, the letter is built without a dangling, awkward sentence in its place — the tool skips it cleanly rather than leaving an empty gap.

Tab 4

Hourly ↔ Salary Converter

Converts between an hourly wage and an equivalent annual salary, using your actual hours per week and weeks per year (not just a flat assumption).

  1. Choose your direction — hourly to salary, or salary to hourly.
  2. Enter the amount (your hourly rate, or your annual salary, depending on direction).
  3. Enter your hours per week — the default is often 40, but adjust it if yours is different.
  4. Enter your weeks worked per year if you don't work all 52 (for example, if you take unpaid time off).
  5. Read the converted amount.
Good to know: The default assumption of a standard 40-hour, 52-week year is common but not universal — always double check the hours and weeks fields match your actual situation before trusting the converted number.
