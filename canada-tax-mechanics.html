How We Handle Ontario's Surtax and Quebec's Rules

Two of the trickier corners of Canadian payroll tax, and exactly how our calculator gets them right — including a bug in our own first version that a wrong number would have hidden.

Ontario's surtax isn't part of the tax brackets

Ontario's published tax brackets top out at 13.16%. But once your provincial tax bill itself crosses two thresholds, Ontario adds a surtax on top — a tax on the tax, calculated separately from the income brackets:

  • 20% surtax on provincial tax over $5,818
  • An additional 16% surtax on provincial tax over $7,446 (36% combined with the first tier)

Here's a concrete example at $200,000 of taxable income:

StepAmount
Basic provincial tax (brackets only)$18,444
Ontario surtax on top$6,484
Total provincial tax$24,928

Both surtax tiers apply to the original basic tax figure independently — not to each other. That distinction sounds pedantic until you get it wrong.

Caught in testing

Our first version calculated the two surtax tiers sequentially — add the 20% tier, then calculate the 36% tier off the already-increased number. That's compounding, and it's wrong: it produced a 21.48% marginal rate at the top of the income scale, when the correct figure (verified against published references) is 20.53%. The fix was one line — both tiers now calculate off the same original basic-tax figure — but the bug would have quietly overstated tax for every high earner in Ontario if we hadn't checked the math against a known reference point before shipping.

Quebec isn't just "another province" in the math

Quebec collects its own provincial income tax separately from the rest of Canada, which triggers a federal abatement: Quebec residents get their federal tax bill reduced by 16.5%, since the federal government isn't collecting a province-level tax on Quebec's behalf the way it does elsewhere.

Quebec also runs its own version of CPP (called QPP) and its own parental insurance program (QPIP) instead of the standard federal EI. Our calculator applies Quebec's own provincial tax brackets and the 16.5% federal abatement correctly, but uses standard CPP/CPP2 rates as an approximation for QPP rather than modeling Quebec's plan as a fully separate system — QPP rates have historically tracked very closely with CPP, but they aren't guaranteed to be identical every year. We say this directly in the calculator's own disclaimer rather than quietly treating Quebec like every other province.
