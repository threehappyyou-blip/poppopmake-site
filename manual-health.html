Tool 1

BMI Calculator

Works out your Body Mass Index from height and weight, and tells you which WHO category it falls into.

  1. Open the tool. Go to the BMI Calculator page.
  2. Pick your units. Click "Metric (kg / cm)" or "Imperial (lb / ft·in)" at the top of the form — the input fields below switch to match.
  3. Enter your weight and height. The result updates as you type; there's no separate "Calculate" button.
  4. Read your BMI and category in the panel below — a number, a category label (Underweight, Normal weight, Overweight, or one of three Obese classes), and a marker on the color-coded scale showing where you land.
  5. Turn on Asian thresholds if they apply to you. Check "Use Asian BMI thresholds" to switch to the WHO's separate Asian-population cutoffs, which classify "overweight" and "obese" starting at lower BMI values than the global standard. The number itself doesn't change — only which category it falls into.
Good to know: BMI is a screening number, not a diagnosis — it can't tell muscle from fat, so it can misclassify very muscular or very lean people, older adults, and pregnant women. Use it as one data point, not the whole picture.

Tool 2

Calorie / TDEE Calculator

Estimates your BMR (calories burned at rest) and TDEE (total daily calories burned), then shows calorie targets for five different goals.

  1. Open the tool. Go to the Calorie Calculator page.
  2. Select your sex using the Female / Male toggle — the underlying formula (Mifflin-St Jeor) uses different constants for each.
  3. Enter your age.
  4. Pick your units and enter your weight and height, the same way as the BMI calculator.
  5. Choose your activity level from the five options, from Sedentary (little or no exercise) to Extra active (very hard exercise or a physical job). Each description gives a concrete example so you're not guessing which one fits.
  6. Read your results. BMR and TDEE appear as two big numbers, followed by a table of five calorie targets: lose weight (faster or steady), maintain, and gain weight (steady or faster), each with its approximate weekly rate of change.
  7. Watch for the warning box. If any target in the table would put you below a general low-calorie guidance threshold, that row is marked and a note explains why — it's shown, not hidden, but flagged rather than treated as a normal target to aim for.
Good to know: The calorie targets use a widely-used rule of thumb (about 3,500 calories per pound of weight change), not an exact physiological law — real results vary with water weight and metabolic adaptation. Treat the numbers as a starting point to adjust from, not a fixed prescription.
