        Redact PDF

        Black out an ID number, a signature, anything sensitive — draw a box over it, and it's permanently removed, not just covered up. Good for the back digits of a resident registration number, an account number, or anything else you'd redact before sharing a document.

        Drag a PDF here, or
        Page 1 of 1
        Find & black out automatically
        This searches the PDF's real text, so it can't see inside scanned pages. Boxes are placed by estimating where each word sits — flip through the pages to check them (and drag an extra box if one falls short) before you download.
        Click and drag over the area you want to hide. Black boxes are permanent once you download — the covered content is removed, not layered over.
        Works best on scanned or photographed documents (the most common case for something like an ID card). Documents with unusual embedded fonts can occasionally render a line of text incorrectly — if a page doesn't look right, zoom in and check before drawing your box, since the box has to be over the real content to work. "Add text" writes new text into the page's pixels — it doesn't edit the document's real, underlying text (that would need a full PDF editor), so it can't reflow existing content, only add or cover things.

        AI Eraser

        Paint over something in the photo and it gets removed, with the AI filling in what was probably behind it.

        Drag an image here, or
        Brush size
        Paint over what you want removed (a little beyond its edges), then press Erase. The AI repaints a 512×512 patch around what you painted and pastes it back, so the rest of your photo stays untouched at full resolution. Good for removing a person, a sign, or an object from an otherwise photo-realistic background; very large areas come out soft.

        How this works: Merge, split, and image-to-PDF use a JavaScript library (pdf-lib) that runs entirely on your device. Remove Background uses a separate open-source AI model (Transformers.js + MODNet) that downloads once and then also runs entirely in your browser. Redact PDF renders each page to an image using PDF.js (the same engine Firefox uses to display PDFs), so the exported file has no underlying text layer left anywhere in it — there's nothing to select or copy, including in the redacted area, because after export the whole page is a flat picture rather than text plus a black box drawn on top. That's a real trade-off: the output PDF is no longer searchable or copy-pasteable, on any page, not just the redacted one. Extract Text uses Tesseract.js, a browser build of the open-source Tesseract OCR engine — the same model family used in a lot of commercial OCR tools — downloaded once per language and cached after that. Face Mosaic uses Google's MediaPipe Face Detector (the BlazeFace model). Enhance Quality's Fast mode is plain image-resizing math with no AI involved; AI Upscale and AI Eraser use small open-source models (via Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web) that download once and then run entirely on your device — AI Eraser's model is roughly 200MB, so that first download is worth doing on Wi-Fi. Face and object detection can miss things, especially at odd angles or low light — always look over a result before sharing it. Nothing is ever uploaded to a server — that also means very large files (100+ MB) may be slow, since your device is doing all the work. There's no file-size cap and no daily limit either way: process one file or a hundred, today or every day. After converting images to a PDF, extracting pages, or removing a background, you can send the result straight into another tool without downloading it first.
