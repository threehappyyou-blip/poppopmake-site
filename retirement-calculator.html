Worked example

With the defaults above — starting at age 30 with a $20,000 balance and a $70,000 salary, contributing 6% of pay with a 50% employer match up to 6%, a 7% annual return, and 3% annual raises — year one works like this: you contribute $4,200 (6% of $70,000), your employer adds $2,100 (50% of that same 6%), and the combined $6,300 added to your $20,000 starting balance grows by $1,841 at 7%, ending year one at $28,141. Repeat that loop every year — salary, contribution, and match dollars all rising 3% a year — for all 35 years until age 65, and the projected balance is $1,538,596: about $253,941 from your own contributions, $126,970 from your employer's match, and $1,137,685 from investment growth alone.

Frequently asked questions

How exactly does the employer match get calculated?

The tool takes the smaller of your contribution percentage or the match cap percentage, then multiplies by your match rate. With the defaults (6% contribution, matched 50% up to a 6% cap), your entire 6% is eligible, so your employer adds 50 cents per dollar you contribute — $2,100 on a $70,000 salary in year one. Contribute more than the cap and the extra goes unmatched; contribute less and only that smaller amount gets matched.

What IRS contribution limit does this use, and does my example hit it?

It uses the 2026 IRS 401(k) limits: a $24,500 base limit, an $8,000 catch-up for ages 50–59 and 64+, and the SECURE 2.0 super catch-up of $11,250 for ages 60–63 specifically. In the default example your desired contribution never gets close to any of these — even in the final projected year (age 64) it's only around $11,474, well under that year's $32,500 limit — but raise the salary or contribution percentage enough and the tool will cap you at the limit for your age.

Is the final balance shown in today's dollars or future dollars?

Today's dollars — the projection labels itself that way. That effectively treats the 7% return assumption as an inflation-adjusted return rather than a raw nominal one, and it's why the IRS contribution limit is held flat across all 35 years instead of being projected upward for future inflation.

Why might my real 401(k) balance end up different from this projection?

This is a projection, not a promise. Real investment returns vary year to year instead of compounding at a flat 7%, and the tool doesn't model vesting schedules on your employer match, investment fees, or the taxes you'll eventually pay on withdrawal — all of which change your real number.

Does this calculator send my salary or balance anywhere?

No. The entire projection runs in your browser; nothing you enter is transmitted to or stored on a server.
