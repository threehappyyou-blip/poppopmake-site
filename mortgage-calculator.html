Worked example

With the defaults above — a $400,000 home price and a $40,000 down payment (10%, which is why PMI applies) — the loan amount is $360,000. At 6.5% over 30 years, the standard amortization formula gives a monthly principal & interest payment of $2,275. Add PMI of $180/month (0.6%/year × $360,000 ÷ 12, since the down payment is under 20%), property tax of $367/month (1.1%/year × $400,000 ÷ 12), and $125/month insurance ($1,500/year ÷ 12) — no HOA dues in this example — and the full estimated monthly payment comes to $2,947. Over the full 30-year term, this loan would accrue $459,160 in total interest.

Frequently asked questions

Why is PMI showing up in my estimate?

PMI appears whenever your down payment is under 20% of the home price — in the default example that's a 10% down payment on a $400,000 home. It's calculated as (loan amount × PMI rate) ÷ 12. It typically cancels once you reach 20–22% equity, but this tool doesn't track that timeline for you — you'll need to watch your own payoff progress and ask your servicer to remove it once you qualify.

How is the monthly principal & interest payment actually calculated?

It's the standard fixed-rate amortization formula, applied to your loan amount (home price minus down payment), your monthly interest rate, and the total number of monthly payments over your loan term — the same math a lender uses to build an amortization schedule. It covers principal and interest only; tax, insurance, PMI, and HOA are added separately in the breakdown below.

Does this include closing costs, discount points, or extra principal payments?

No. This estimate covers only the recurring monthly cost — principal & interest, tax, insurance, PMI, and HOA dues. It doesn't model one-time closing costs, paying points to buy down your rate, or how extra principal payments would shorten your payoff timeline.

Why might my real lender quote be different from this number?

Property tax and insurance here are whatever rate you enter, not your actual county or insurer's bill — property tax alone typically runs somewhere around 0.3%–2.2% of home value per year depending on the state, so adjust both fields to your real numbers for a closer estimate. Your lender's actual quote will also reflect underwriting details this tool doesn't see.

Does this calculator send my loan details anywhere?

No. Every calculation runs locally in your browser as you type — nothing is sent to a server, logged, or stored after you close the tab.
