9 States Compared: Where Your Paycheck Goes Furthest

2026 tax year

Same salary, same job, same filing status — and yet a $65,000 earner in Texas takes home about $3,000 more per year than the exact same earner in California. Here's the real math, state by state.

Federal tax and FICA are the same no matter where you live. State tax is the wildcard, and it varies more than most people expect — from nothing at all to a genuinely complex bracket system layered with its own extra payroll taxes. Below is every state our paycheck calculator currently covers, ranked from lightest to heaviest tax burden, using a single filer earning $65,000 as the benchmark.

No state income tax Flat-rate state tax Progressive bracket state tax

The three tax-free states: Texas, Florida, Washington

These three charge $0 in state income tax, period. A $65,000 single filer keeps $54,408 of it after federal tax and FICA — nothing extra comes off for the state. That's roughly a 16.3% total effective tax rate, the lowest of any state we cover. The trade-off usually shows up elsewhere (sales tax, property tax), but that's outside what a paycheck calculator can see.

The flat-rate middle: Pennsylvania, Colorado, Illinois

These three each apply a single percentage to your income, no brackets involved. Pennsylvania is the lightest at 3.07%, applied straight to gross wages with no deduction. Colorado (4.4%) and Illinois (4.95%, the highest flat rate among the three) both apply their rate to your federal taxable income instead, which softens the impact slightly. At $65,000, the gap between all three flat-rate states is under 1 percentage point of total effective tax.

New Jersey's extra layer: SDI and FLI

New Jersey's income tax brackets alone (1.4% to 10.75%) aren't dramatically different from Pennsylvania's flat rate at moderate incomes. What pushes New Jersey slightly higher is two payroll taxes most other states don't have: State Disability Insurance and Family Leave Insurance, adding roughly 0.35% combined on top of income tax.

The progressive heavyweights: New York and California

Both states use their own multi-bracket systems layered on top of federal tax, and both land at the top of this list. California adds a mandatory 1.3% State Disability Insurance tax with no wage cap at all — everyone pays it, on every dollar. New York's bracket structure alone pushes a $65,000 earner past the 20% mark before FICA is even counted separately in the comparison. Both remain the most expensive states on this list at every income level we've tested.
