The Four-Year Window Where Your 401(k) Limit Jumps Higher

Most people know there's a bigger 401(k) limit once you turn 50. Fewer know there's a second, even bigger jump at 60 — and that it disappears again at 64. It's easy to miss because it doesn't last.

2026 contribution limits by age

AgeBase limitCatch-upTotal
Under 50$24,500—$24,500
50 – 59$24,500+$8,000$32,500
60 – 63$24,500+$11,250$35,750
64+$24,500+$8,000$32,500

That middle row isn't a typo, and it isn't a continuation of the 50+ catch-up. It's a separate, higher "super catch-up" that SECURE 2.0 created specifically for ages 60 through 63 — and then the limit drops back down to the ordinary catch-up amount at 64. If you're picturing the limit as a staircase that goes up once at 50 and stays there, it actually goes up twice and back down once.

What the narrow window is worth

The super catch-up gives you $3,250 more room per year than the standard 50+ catch-up — only during those four years. Maxed out in all four:

$3,250 × 4 years = $13,000 in extra contribution room that exists in no other age range, including the years right after it.

Invested rather than left in cash, that extra room compounds like everything else in the account. At a 7% annual return — the same planning assumption our own retirement calculator uses, not a promise — that $13,000 grows to roughly $17,000 after just four more years, before accounting for anything invested in the surrounding years at all.

Why this is easy to miss

Most explanations of catch-up contributions describe a single threshold ("50 and older"), which was true before 2025 and is still directionally true for most of the age range — just not for these specific four years. A generic answer, including from an AI assistant without current, jurisdiction-specific training, is likely to miss the second jump entirely and quote the plain 50+ figure straight through to retirement.
