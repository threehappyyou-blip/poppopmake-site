      Each issue, and the fastest fix

      Long sentences

      Readers lose the thread after about 25 words. Split at the first “and”, “which” or comma that starts a new idea. Keep one long sentence if it builds to a point; don’t keep three in a row.

      Passive voice

      “The report was approved by Sam” → “Sam approved the report.” Put the person doing the action first. Leave passive voice when the doer truly doesn’t matter (“the store opens at 9” style facts, lab methods).

      Fillers and hedges

      Words like very, really, just, basically add length but not meaning; maybe, probably, I think make you sound unsure. Delete them and read the sentence again — it almost always gets stronger.

      Wordy phrases

      The checker suggests the short form: in order to → to, due to the fact that → because, at this point in time → now, has the ability to → can, prior to → before.

      Clichés

      “At the end of the day”, “think outside the box”, “game changer” — readers skim past them. Replace each with the specific thing you mean.

      Repeated words and sentence openings

      The same content word twice within a dozen words, or three sentences in a row starting with “The”, makes prose feel clumsy. Use a pronoun, a synonym, or reorder one sentence.

      A 60-second checklist before you hit send

      1. Run the checker and fix every orange (very long) sentence.
      2. Turn passive sentences active wherever you know who did it.
      3. Delete fillers; keep hedges only where you genuinely aren’t sure.
      4. Swap each wordy phrase for its short form.
      5. Read the first sentence of each paragraph alone — together they should tell the whole story.
      6. Run your spell-check last.

      Where this checker stops

      • Not a grammar or spelling checker. It won’t catch “their/there” or a missing comma.
      • Pattern-based, not AI. Rules are simple and visible, so it can’t understand context — a flagged passive or repeat is a prompt to look, not an order to change.
      • English only.
      • Not an AI detector. It doesn’t judge who or what wrote the text (why).

      Frequently asked questions

      How do I check the quality of my writing for free?

      Paste your draft into the box above and press “Check quality”. You get a score out of 100 and every issue highlighted in your text with a suggested fix. There’s no sign-up or word limit, and your text stays in your browser.

      What makes writing high quality?

      Clear writing says one idea per sentence, uses active verbs, cuts words that add no meaning, varies sentence length, and avoids stock phrases. Those are exactly the things this checker measures; correct grammar and spelling are the other half.

      Is a writing quality checker the same as a grammar checker?

      No. A grammar checker looks for errors (agreement, spelling, punctuation). A quality checker looks at clarity and style — sentence length, passive voice, filler, clichés and repetition. Error-free text can still be hard to read, so it helps to use both.

      What is a good writing quality score?

      On this checker, 80 or above reads well and 90+ is polished. Academic and technical writing often lands lower because of longer sentences and more passive voice, which is normal for that genre.

      Can I use it for essays, emails and blog posts?

      Yes. It works on any English text. For essays, also check the Writing Style Analyzer’s academic column; for emails and blog posts, aim for 80+ and short paragraphs.
